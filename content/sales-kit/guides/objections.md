title: Answering common objections
---

Every playbook has objections for its own group. These come up with everyone. Listen first, ask one question back, then answer in a sentence or two.

## "It's too expensive."

Ask what they pay today, all in: hosting, the site builder, bookings, email, forms, the designer who fixes things. One monthly price usually replaces three or four bills. If it really is too much, offer a smaller plan now and the bigger one when they grow.

## "I can do it myself with a free builder."

They can, and many do. Ask how much time they spend on it each month and what that time is worth. You look after updates, security, backups and support, and their site is a real WordPress site they own and can move.

## "I already have a website."

Ask when it was last updated, whether it books, sells or takes payments, and how it looks on a phone. Offer a free website check: {{brand}} sends a short report they can keep, whatever they decide.

## "I need to think about it."

Ask what they need to think through, and answer that one thing. Then agree a date to follow up, and send a one-page summary they can share.

## "I'm locked into a contract."

Ask when it ends and put a reminder in your calendar a month before. Offer to have the new site ready so they can switch the day the old one ends.

## "What if I want to leave?"

Their content is theirs. They can export the site and move it, and there is no long contract.

## "Is it secure?"

Every site runs on its own, with its own database, nightly backups, updates handled for them and a free security certificate.

## "Who do I call when something breaks?"

You. Give them {{support}} and a reply time you can keep.

## "I've been burned by a web designer before."

Show the live demo and set the site up before they pay, so they see exactly what they get. Keep prices fixed and written down.
